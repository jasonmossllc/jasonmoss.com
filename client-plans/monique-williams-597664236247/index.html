Section 02

Where you are right now

You're not behind. You're at the very start of a new chapter — and in a lot of ways, we're building this from the ground up. That's not a problem. It's an advantage, as long as we get the foundations right.

Let me start with what's genuinely strong here, because there's more of it than you might give yourself credit for.

What's working

  • Your gift is connection. You're intuitive, you read people, and when you're in a real conversation you make someone feel seen, heard and understood. That isn't a small thing — it's the whole thing. It's the reason the people who work with you tend to stay.
  • One-to-one is where you shine. You told me that when your list was small you'd speak to people one to one, and you could nearly always sign someone up for a course or a smaller offer that way. That tracks. Intimacy is your natural selling environment, and most people never find theirs.
  • You already have a warm audience to draw on. You have an old list and a network — most people relaunching "almost from scratch" have neither. Aimed at the right people, that's a real head start: once you choose your niche, some of the people who fit it may already be in there.
  • The people who buy from you, buy everything. Your words: "a lot of clients bought literally everything I offered." That's rare. It tells me the trust is real and the delivery is real.
  • You love to teach in writing. Blog posts, newsletters, little insights and reflections — you said that's what feels most like you. That matters, because it means your content engine is something you already enjoy. We don't have to manufacture the habit. We just have to point it in the right direction.
  • You have real material and a track record. Your years facilitating Bob Proctor's Thinking Into Results gave you a body of work people loved. We're not starting from nothing.

What's holding you back

Now the honest part — because this is where the actual leverage is.

  • Your niche is too broad to call anyone in. Right now you describe your work as helping "women who feel called to create, share or build something that feels meaningful to them." I understand exactly what you mean by it. But it's too wide and too vague to land with any one specific person. When you speak to everyone, no one quite feels you're speaking to them.
  • There's no clear, tangible transformation yet. You've been selling coaching and deep inner work, rather than a specific, named journey from where someone is to where they want to be. That's almost always what's underneath "the bigger coaching packages were a struggle." You said selling felt like "pushing a boulder up hill" and your sales copy "never felt quite aligned." This is exactly why.
  • Lead flow is low — and it's the real constraint. Right now the newsletter is essentially your only channel, and there's no steady stream of new people coming into your world. Almost everything in this plan is built to solve that.
  • You've been performing a version of yourself. A client told you, lovingly, that the "you" she got on coaching calls wasn't the "you" in your marketing. You found that when you let yourself just be yourself, it was easier to sign people up — but that "flew in the face of the marketing tactics" you'd been told to use. So you went quiet and inconsistent. That gap between the real you and the marketed you has been quietly costing you.
  • The old reflex was to sell another thing. In your words: "there was never a progressive higher level of support — I was always trying to just find another way to sell something, hence so many courses were created." That sprawl is exhausting, and it scatters the very energy your magic runs on. We're going to do the opposite.

Your magic was never the problem. What's been missing is a container for it — a specific person, a tangible transformation, and one clear offer — so all that intuition and care finally has somewhere to land.

Section 03

The biggest opportunities I see

If you focus on only a few things over the next stretch, focus on these. Every one of them is pulled straight from what I saw going through your business.

1. Get specific about who you serve

This is the foundation everything else sits on, which is why it comes first. The moment you go from "women who feel called to create something meaningful" to a specific person in a specific situation, your message gets sharper, your content gets easier to write, and your offer almost designs itself. You don't need it perfect — you need to pick a direction and commit to it long enough to learn. This single move unlocks more than any tactic I could hand you.

2. Talk to the right people — market research inside your niche

Once you've chosen your niche, the next move is real conversations with people who actually fit it — not just whoever happens to be on your old list. Sometimes those people are already in your network or audience, and that's a head start most people relaunching don't have. Sometimes you'll need to go and find new people, outside your warm circle, who match the niche. Either way the filter is the same: talk to people who are genuinely your person, so you can hear — in their own words, and on a much deeper level — what they're struggling with and what they want. That's how we find your message instead of guessing at it. And some of those conversations quietly turn into "can I work with you?"

3. Build one clear, high-ticket one-to-one offer

Instead of a sprawl of courses, one offer: a one-to-one container built around a specific transformation, taking someone from where they are to where they want to be. When the destination is clear and genuinely desirable, and you're offering it to people who can actually pay, the bigger sale stops feeling like pushing a boulder uphill. The struggle you've had with bigger packages was never a you problem. It's a "what's actually being sold" problem — and that's fixable.

4. Wrap the deep work in something tangible

Here's the key to selling the spiritual, intuitive work you love. Most people aren't walking around saying they want to "align with their spiritual side," even when that's exactly what they need most. So we lead with the tangible, real-world thing they already want, and let the deeper work be the path that gets them there. Think of it as a doorway: meet them in the worldly desire, then take them into the depth on the inside. Get this right and you can finally sell the work that matters most to you — by meeting people exactly where they already are.

5. Choose one channel to reach new people

Your newsletter is wonderful and we're keeping it — but it mostly nurtures people who already know you. On its own it won't bring new people in. You told me you're not drawn to social media, and I'm not going to force it. But you are open to YouTube, and that's the opportunity: YouTube can put you in front of people who've never heard of you, and once they find you, you bring them onto your list and deepen the relationship from there. We don't switch this on first. But it's the growth engine waiting for you the moment the foundations are set.

The core shift

You are not selling coaching, and you are not selling spirituality. You are taking a specific person from where they are to where they want to be — and your intuitive, soulful work is how you get them there.

Everything in the next sections flows from that one sentence. Read it twice.

Section 04

What needs to shift first

Before any tactic works, these have to change. Tactics on a shaky foundation just make the shaky thing louder.

From broad and vague to specific and tangible

This is the big one. Move from "women who feel called to create something meaningful" to a specific person and a specific, tangible gap between where they are and where they want to be. Broad feels safe because it keeps your options open. But it's the very thing keeping your message from landing. Specific is what calls people in.

From selling coaching to selling a transformation

Stop selling "coaching" or "sessions," and start selling a clear journey with a destination people actually want. People don't buy coaching — they buy where it takes them. When you can name the A and the B and show that you're the bridge between them, the sale gets dramatically easier. This is the real fix for the boulder-uphill feeling you described.

From a group workshop to one-to-one — for now

I know the pull toward a group workshop, especially with the time pressure of the part-time job. But I'm going to steer you away from it at this stage, and I want to be clear about why. Your single biggest constraint right now is lead flow — you don't yet have enough new people coming in to fill a group well. So we focus on the offer that earns the most from the fewest leads, and that's one-to-one. Group is a beautiful next step. It's just not the now step.

From intuition without structure to intuition with structure

Your intuition and your relational magic are the gift, and we are never sacrificing them. But on their own, with no clear model underneath, they don't reliably turn into income. The work here is to give that intuition a structure to live inside: a clear who, a clear offer, a clear path. The structure doesn't cage the magic. It's what lets the magic actually reach people — and pay you.

From a marketing persona to simply being you

You already discovered this. When you stopped "presenting" yourself a certain way and just let people meet the real you, it was easier to sign them up. That's not a small insight — that's the whole strategy. The version of you that shows up on a great coaching call is the version that needs to show up in your writing. We throw out any "tactic" that requires you to be someone you're not. Being yourself isn't just more honest. For you, it converts better.

Hold this: lead flow is the constraint that matters most right now. Every recommendation in this plan is built to solve it — which is exactly why we start with one-to-one and one channel, not a group and a content empire.

Section 07

Marketing & content strategy

Foundations first, visibility second. But here's where the visibility goes when it's time.

One principle before any tactic: marketing is step two. We get the who, the message, and the offer right first — otherwise we're just driving traffic to something blurry. With that said, here's the shape of it.

Keep the newsletter — and write it as you

Your newsletter is the right home base. You love it, it's sustainable, it keeps things simple, and it lets you teach in writing, which is what feels most like you. Keep it. Two changes. First, write it as the real you — the little stories, the rants with the funny clinchers, the deeper spiritual reflections — not a polished marketing version of you. Second, when you're ready, lift the rhythm. Weekly built the habit; I'd like you moving toward three times a week or more, because frequency is what keeps you in someone's world. Just be clear on what the newsletter is for: it nurtures the people who already found you. It is not, on its own, how new people find you.

YouTube is your way to reach new people

This is the growth piece, and it's the one channel I'd point you toward — because you're genuinely open to it and it fits you. You don't like social media, and I'm not going to make you do it. But YouTube is a different animal: it's teaching, it's long-form, and it rewards exactly the kind of thoughtful, soulful sharing you already do in your writing. Someone finds a video, feels you, joins your list — and now the newsletter has someone new to nurture. That's the whole engine: YouTube brings them in, the newsletter deepens it, the conversation converts. We turn this on once the foundations are set, not before.

Your research conversations are marketing too

Don't miss this one. The market research calls aren't only research. They're real, human, one-to-one conversations — your single greatest strength — and once they're with the right, niche-fit people, some of them will naturally become your first clients. So in these early weeks, your "marketing" and your "research" are the same activity. That's the most aligned possible way for you to begin.

Section 08

Systems & operations

At this stage, the right system is the simplest one you'll actually keep.

Your two real constraints are time — you've still got the part-time job — and perfectionism, which you named yourself as the thing that pulls you out of alignment. So the operating principle for this season is simple: fewer things, done consistently, beats more things done perfectly. We are not building a complicated machine.

What to keep simple

  • One offer, not a catalog. The old pattern was to create another course every time you needed a sale. We're done with that. One one-to-one offer, one message, one channel. Here, simplicity is the strategy — not a limitation.
  • A rhythm you can sustain. A consistent newsletter you actually send beats a perfect one you agonize over and skip. Done and consistent. Every time.
  • A light way to book conversations. Early on, your "system" is mostly a list of people, a short personal message, and a calendar link. That's it. Don't over-build it.

Protect against the two things that derail you

  • Perfectionism. This is your known trigger. When you feel the urge to polish endlessly before publishing, treat that as the signal to ship it and move on. Imperfect and out in the world beats perfect and stuck in drafts.
  • The urge to sell another new thing. When money feels tight, the old reflex is to invent a new offer. Resist it. The answer is more conversations and more visibility for the one offer — never a new one.

About the part-time job

Keep it for now. The whole plan is built to fit around it — one-to-one clients, a newsletter, and research calls are all low-overhead and flexible. The job comes off the table when the business can genuinely replace it, which is exactly what your $10K-a-month goal represents. We're building toward that on purpose, not gambling your rent on it.

Your 90-day action plan

Everything above, sequenced into something you can start on Monday. The order is the strategy — don't rearrange it.

  1. Go through Nail Your Niche first. Brainstorm directions and commit to at least one that meets the workshop's criteria and feels like you. Message it to me on Telegram so we can refine it together. Nothing else moves until this is chosen — it decides who you talk to next.

Days 31–60 Build the offer and find the message
  1. Run your 8 to 12 conversations. Talk to those niche-fit people, and listen hard. Capture their exact words — what they're struggling with, what they want, how they describe it. This is the raw material of your message.

Days 61–90 Lead flow and your first clients
  1. Invite people into the offer. Some of your research conversations are already warm — make the natural, no-pressure invitation to work with you one-to-one. Enroll your first clients.
  2. Launch YouTube, simply. Start teaching and sharing the way you already love to in writing — no production, just you. Point viewers to your newsletter so new people start entering your world.
  3. Keep refining the message. Every conversation sharpens it. Send me what you're learning on Telegram so we can adjust as you go.
  4. Hold the line. One offer. One channel. No new courses. When the urge to add something or polish endlessly hits, that's the signal to stay the course instead.
