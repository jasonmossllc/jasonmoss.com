Section 02

Where you are right now

You've spent ten years building the things most coaches never get: a real point of view, a real community, and people who come back. The next chapter is about pointing all of it at one person.

Let me start with what's true and good, because there's a lot of it and I don't want it to get buried under the rest.

What's working

People have always been the core of what you do. Nurse. Prison service. Teaching English in Sri Lanka. Running a pub. Ten years of coaching. You described it as "a varied career, but always about the people," and that's not a résumé line. That's the pattern. Community, connection, people in a room together: that's what you want at the center of this, and it's also your superpower. Everything in this plan is built to keep it there.

You have a philosophy with a name and a shape. The Inside-Out Way: check in, respond, show up. A published book. A brand, Living Inside-Out, that you're putting joy at the heart of. Most coaches at your audience size have a vibe. You have a method, and you can teach it.

You build rooms people want to be in. A Circle of Joy. A Long Table Dinner where twenty people sit down to a roast together. Reconnect on Tuesday mornings. You told me word of mouth and the long table events are where your leads come from today. That's community working as a growth engine, and it's rare. We're going to lean on it harder, not less.

You close. Your words: "tend to close when I do have them." So your sales problem isn't persuasion. It's volume. That's a much better problem to have, and it's the one this plan solves.

Your mindset is in good shape. You rate it an 8. You're in clinical supervision monthly, you read, you stay around people on the same path. You said the money blocks "seem to be shifting," and you said this to your accountant: self-worth and wisdom, now the how. I believe you. The how is what follows.

And you've already named the last chapter: "the chapter when I thought I just needed to create one more thing." Naming it is most of the way out of it. You'll notice this plan asks you to create almost nothing new.

What's holding you back

Here's the honest read. None of it is a talent problem or a quality problem, and I want you to hear that before the list.

  • The engine isn't built yet. NZ$3,554 of revenue over six months, against a goal of $10K months. I'm not saying that to deflate you. I'm saying it so we're both clear that we aren't tuning something that already works. We're building the thing that will. That's a different kind of work, and it's very doable from where you stand.
  • Not enough new people are meeting you. 701 followers on Instagram and just under 500 on email. Top of funnel is the biggest single constraint in the business right now, and it's where most of the next 90 days goes.
  • The message points at the destination, not at the person. Your homepage: "Joy? Confidence? Freedom, Flow? What would you choose?" Your bio: "From stuck to truly alive." Your highlights: Joy, Dates With Life, Adventures, The magic. All of it is beautiful, and all of it describes where someone ends up. Almost none of it describes where they are on the day they'd finally pay for help.
  • Three different "whos." Your assessment says adults in midlife who've lost touch with what brings them joy. Your Work Together page says "those struggling with self-doubt/shame/imposter syndrome." Your Instagram says stuck to alive. And you're drawn to LinkedIn because of people "caught up with work/corporate life." Each one is a real clue. Side by side, a stranger can't tell who this is for.
  • The offers aren't connected. You said it yourself: "my offers aren't connected through a clear customer journey." Three paid doors, three free ones, and the price of the main one "varies by package." The most recent 1:1 sale was $900 for six weeks, while your 12-week programme sits at $2,975.
  • Content volume and reach. Several reels a week or fewer, and most of them reaching a small number of people. Not enough volume to grow, and not yet built for the way Instagram decides what to push.
  • Zero sales calls a week. Not because people say no. Because the path to a conversation isn't there yet.

Every one of those is downstream of one decision. That's the good news, and it's the next section.

You don't have a confidence problem, a content problem, or a call-to-action problem. You have one upstream decision to make, and almost everything in this plan gets easier once it's made.

Section 03

The biggest opportunities I see

If you only focus on a few things over the next 90 days, focus on these. In this order.

1. Decide who needs this, not who would enjoy it

Aliveness is beautiful. The question I keep coming back to is: for whom is aliveness a need-to-have? People don't buy coaching because they resonate with a point of view. They buy it because there's a gap in their life that's creating real discomfort, and they have a strong desire to close it. So who is in enough pain from a life that lacks joy that they've reached the moment of "enough is enough, I need to change this"? Burnout. Loneliness. Flatness. Going through the motions. That person is your person, and that's the person we build this business around. This is the foundation. Everything else in the plan stands on it.

2. Meet them where they are, then invite them to joy

Your content isn't landing lightly because it's wrong. It's going over people's heads because it starts at the end of the journey. The bridge is simple to describe and takes real craft to do: speak to the present state first, make someone feel seen, heard, and met in their disconnection, and then open the door to a joy-filled life and what's possible on the other side. Apply that one change to your reels, your stories, your bio, and your website, and you have the single biggest messaging lever available to you.

3. Turn Instagram into a top-of-funnel engine

At 701 followers, the number has to come up, and it'll happen through organic or paid. While the messaging is still being refined, organic is the right path. That means one reel a day minimum, and treating each one as data: what made people stop, what made them keep watching, what made them share. Study it, double down on what works, and the reach compounds. I'd put most of your marketing energy here. It's a learning loop, not a performance.

4. Simplify the path to one front door, one mid door, one back door

You have more doors than a business this size needs, and that's an easy fix. You only need one back door: your 1:1 coaching. Your front door is your reels and your low-priced in-person experiences. Your mid door is one live event a month. That's the whole customer journey you've been asking for. No need to overcomplicate it.

5. Price the back door like the thing that moves the business

Your 1:1 is where the revenue is. Raise its price, hold it steady, and let everything else in the business exist to bring people to it. The math in Section 06 will show you why this matters more than any other single number.

$10K months isn't a bigger version of today's business. It's a narrower one. One person, one path, one back door.

Section 04

What needs to shift first

Before any tactic in this plan works, these have to move. They're not to-dos. They're the ground the to-dos stand on.

From "one more thing" to the same few things, every day

You have a book, a method, a membership, three event formats, two free guides, a weekly email, and a free session anyone can get by DM. That's plenty. The next 90 days don't ask you to build anything new. They ask you to repeat a small number of things until they work. You said you want "to spend less time continually creating and experimenting, and more time building momentum around what works." Good. This is that.

From vision-led to person-led marketing

You're oriented around vision and desire. In the coaching room, that's a gift. In the marketing, it needs a partner: an honest, specific picture of what someone's life looks like before they find you. Not to dwell there. To meet them there. The vision is the destination. The person is the starting point. Marketing that takes someone on a journey begins where they are.

From "stronger calls to action" to a clearer need

You listed stronger CTAs as the fix more than once in your assessment. I don't think you have a call-to-action problem. Your bio already says "DM PITSTOP for free session." Your site says "Book a call" five different ways. A call to action works when the person reading it has already recognized themselves in what came before. That recognition is the work. Once it's there, the CTAs you already have will start doing their job.

From creative-and-responsive to planned-and-repeated

Your words: "more creative and responsive than strategic," and "going with a last minute idea and scraping it together at short notice." You also said that's changing, and that you feel more able to commit to regular events now. I take you at your word, and the plan is built for it. There's a weekly rhythm in Section 08, and most of it is the same week, repeated. The spontaneity doesn't go anywhere. It goes inside the rhythm instead of replacing it.

From measuring nothing to watching three numbers

You wrote: "I'm not effectively tracking what's working." Three numbers fix that. Hook rate, retention, shares. The Edits app shows you all three for every reel. Looking at them once a week is the difference between twelve weeks of posting and twelve weeks of learning.

You have a philosophy. What's missing is the bridge into a specific group of people who need it. That's not a flaw in the philosophy. It's the next piece of work.

Section 05

Positioning & messaging

The B of your transformation is clear and lovely. The A is what we need to write.

What your copy says right now

Here's what a stranger reads today. The homepage: "Joy? Confidence? Freedom, Flow? What would you choose?" Then a stack of maybes: "Maybe you are wanting more freedom, energy, fun. Maybe you are wanting to feel more joy, more peace, a sense of belonging." The Instagram bio: "Leading Inside-Out Coach. 10+yrs coaching, 1000+ lives changed. From stuck to truly alive." The Work Together page: "The most courageous journeys begin within."

Every line is true, and every line is about the destination. "What would you choose?" asks someone to pick a dream. The person who's going to invest in high-ticket coaching isn't choosing a dream. They're trying to get out of something. They have a Tuesday that feels like every other Tuesday, and they've started to wonder if this is just what life is now. Speak to that Tuesday and they'll lean in. Speak to the dream and they'll nod, feel warm, and scroll on. That's what I think is happening with most of your content: it's resonating without converting, because it's speaking so much to the desired state that it hasn't yet built the bridge from where they are.

One thing I noticed on your grid: a reel that opens with "The shit doesn't seem so shit..." Of everything I saw, that's the one that starts where someone actually is. Notice which direction it points.

Writing the A

Your assessment already has the raw material. You wrote that some of your people "have spent years caught up in responsibilities, expectations and putting others first, and have lost touch with what brings them joy." That's a start. Now go further, because that sentence is still a summary. I want the texture:

  • What are the symptoms of having lost touch? What does their day-to-day actually look and feel like? Waking up flat. Being busy and lonely at the same time. Doing everything for everyone and feeling nothing for themselves. Name it the way they'd name it.
  • What's the threshold moment? The point where "something's missing" becomes "enough is enough, I need to change this." That moment is what makes someone seek support and pay for it.
  • Who are they, concretely? Your instinct toward LinkedIn, people "caught up with work/corporate life," is a clue about the person, not just the platform. Hold the platform question for now. Answer the person question first.
  • What's the real urgency? Why now, and not in a year? What is it costing them to stay where they are?

Use your own clients to answer this. The people who've paid you for 1:1: what was happening in their lives the week they booked? Not what they wanted. What they were living in. That's your A, and it's probably more specific than you think.

Then the bridge

Once the A is written, every piece of messaging follows the same shape. Present state first, so they feel seen and met. Then the threshold, so they feel understood. Then, and only then, the invitation to a joy-filled life and what's possible on the other side. The joy doesn't get smaller in this. It gets a doorway.

Meet them where they are first. That's the key to marketing that takes someone on a journey. The joy is the destination. It can't also be the greeting.

Section 06

Offers & pricing

Keep what you've built. Give each piece one job, and let only one of them carry the revenue.

The back door: 1:1 coaching

This is the only back door the business needs right now, and it should be priced like it. Raise it. Here's the math that makes this the most important number in the plan. At $900 a package, $10K months means eleven new clients every single month, which at 701 followers isn't realistic yet. At your 12-week price of $2,975, it's three or four. At a price that reflects a transformation someone needs rather than one they'd enjoy, it's fewer still. One package. One price. Higher than where the 12-week sits today. And then hold it, on every call, so "varies by package" becomes a sentence you don't say anymore.

The front door: Circle of Joy and your in-person experiences

Circle of Joy is at $47, or $37 early bird. Lower it. I want to treat your in-person experiences as the front of the business, a lead-generation activity where the barrier to entry is as low as it can be and the job is to get more people in the door, not to make money in the room. The Long Table at $25 and Reconnect for free already work that way, which is why they're where your leads come from. Make Circle of Joy match. Then make sure every one of these rooms ends with the same next step: a conversation with you, or a seat at the monthly event.

The mid door: one live event a month

This is the new piece, and it's small. One live event a month, online, priced as a low one-off, something like NZ$11 or $22. Its job is two things at once: create the community feel you love, and clearly ascend people into your 1:1. A masterclass, a workshop, a circle, whatever format feels most like you. The format matters less than the rhythm and the invitation at the end. This is where the community energy lives for new people, and it's the bridge between someone enjoying your content and someone booking a call.

The Inside-Out Circle membership

I know how much this community means to you, and I respect that you're keeping it. I also think you've already found its best job, in your own words: "not promoting it outright, but as the after-step." That's exactly right. Let it be home for the people who've already worked with you, the place they stay in touch and come back for a top-up session, which you said already happens. What I'd ask is that it not be a front door or a stepping stone for new people. A $37 membership that's generous enough to feel like the answer makes it very hard for someone to feel they need the full programme, and it offers a lot of the same value as your live experiences. So let the monthly live event do that job for newcomers, and let the Circle do the job it's already doing well: holding your people after the work.

The whole map

Reels and low-priced in-person rooms at the front. One monthly live event in the middle. 1:1 at the back. The free guides, the weekly email, and the PITSTOP session all feed the same path. Nobody has to wonder where to go next, including you.

Section 07

Marketing & content strategy

Reels are the top of the funnel. Stories are the middle. A live event or a conversation is the bottom. That's the pathway. No need to overcomplicate it.

Reels: one a day, built to be pushed

This is where I'd put most of your marketing focus. The volume has to come up: one reel a day, minimum. And the content itself has to change shape. Instagram pushes what stops the scroll, keeps people watching, and makes them want to send it to a friend. So: stronger hooks that name the present state from Section 05. Shorter, headline-style reels with captions on screen and a line or two of body text. A share loop built in at the end, something like "share this with a friend if this resonates." Less of the off-the-cuff reflection on joy, more of the thing a specific person would stop for because it's about their Tuesday.

Download the Edits app, Instagram's own editor, which shows hook rate and retention per reel. Every week, look at what made people stop, what made them stay, and what made them share. Those are the three metrics that matter. Make more of whatever's winning. That's the entire content strategy, and it works because it learns.

Stories: several times a day, for the people who already follow

Once someone follows you, stories are where trust gets built. This is where your testimonials go. This is where you talk about your offers, show your authority and expertise, and invite people, often, into either a conversation or the monthly live event. Several times a day, not several times a week. Stories are your middle of funnel, and right now the middle is quiet.

DMs: every day, with the people who engaged

Reach out to the people who engage with your posts. Conversation first, not a pitch. You asked whether the coaching conversations idea should go in the diary for a week every other month. I'd make it smaller and constant instead: a few messages every day to people who just showed you they're listening. Your free PITSTOP session is a fine door for that. Keep it.

One live event a month

The mid door from Section 06. Invite from reels, from stories, from the weekly email, and from DMs. End it with a clear invitation into 1:1. Then do it again next month. Same format, new theme, better invitation each time.

Email

Keep the weekly email. Just point it at the same person and the same invitation as everything else. One message, one path, every channel.

  • Top of funnel: daily reels, built for stop, stay, share. Judged weekly on those three numbers.
  • Middle of funnel: stories several times a day. Testimonials, offers, invitations.
  • Bottom of funnel: a monthly live event and daily DM conversations, both pointing to a call.
  • Organic first. Once the messaging is proven and reels are consistently getting pushed, paid traffic becomes the next lever. Not before.
Section 08

Systems & operations

Nothing new to build. Rearrange the hours you already have around the three things that matter.

Where the hours go now

Outside the twenty hours of charity work, you have roughly sixteen hours a week in the business. Three on event prep. Three on emails and writing. Four on networking and conversations. Two on coaching. One responding, one on admin. And two on socials. Two. For the channel that's carrying the top of the funnel. That's the imbalance to fix, and it's a reallocation, not an addition. The daily reel, stories, and DMs become the biggest block on your calendar. Event prep shrinks, because the events become repeatable formats, which you said you want anyway, and because a $25 room shouldn't take three hours a week to prepare.

Your VA

You have twenty hours a month of help, and you told me clearly why that matters alongside the charity work and the kind of admin that's hard for you. That's your call, and I'll take it as given. So let's point those hours well: event logistics, scheduling the reels you've filmed, website updates, the steps-heavy things. Your hours go to the things only you can do: filming, stories, conversations, coaching.

A weekly rhythm

Here's the week, repeated:

  • One batch-filming session a week: five to seven reels, shot in one sitting, in the shapes that are working.
  • One reel posted every day. Stories several times a day. DMs every day to people who engaged.
  • One review a week: hook rate, retention, shares for every reel. Rank them. Note what the top ones share.
  • One live event a month, same format, promoted from every channel for the ten days before.
  • One weekly email, pointed at the same person and the same invitation.

One simple scorecard

You wrote that your biggest opportunity in finances is "being more involved with the numbers, and have something to be interested in." Here's something to be interested in. One sheet, five numbers, updated weekly: followers, reel views, DM conversations started, calls booked, clients enrolled. That's it. You just moved accountants and said you can feel something shifting around the money. This is the same muscle, pointed at the business week to week.

Repeatable formats

You said you want "more structured, repeatable programmes and workshop formats, with resources and processes that can be adapted for different audiences without starting from scratch each time." Yes. Circle of Joy gets one structure you reuse, swapping the theme. The monthly live event gets one structure you reuse, swapping the theme. Your 1:1 is already The Inside-Out Way. You've built the tools. The next 90 days are about using the same ones again and again.

Section 09

Your 90-day action plan

Concrete, sequenced, start-Monday. The first thirty days are the only ones that ask you to think hard. The rest is repetition.

Days 1–30 Foundation — decide who, and start the daily reel
  1. Write the A. One page. Who they are, what their days look and feel like when they've lost touch, the symptoms, and the "enough is enough" moment that makes them seek help. Start with your past 1:1 clients: what was happening the week each of them booked? Bring the page to our next call and we'll finish it together. Nothing else in the plan is as important as this.
  2. Download the Edits app and post one reel a day, starting Monday. Shorter, headline-style, captions on screen, a share line at the end. Don't wait for the A to be finished. Start now and let the reels teach you while you write it.
  3. Review the numbers every Friday. Hook rate, retention, and shares for the week's seven reels. Rank them. Write one line on what the top two had in common. That line is next week's brief.
  4. Show up in stories several times a day. A testimonial, something you're offering, an invitation into a conversation. Every day.
  5. Set one 1:1 price. One package, one price, higher than where the 12-week sits today. Decide it, write it down, and bring it to a call so we can pressure-test it together before you say it to anyone.
  6. Message the people who engage. Every day, a few DMs to whoever liked, commented, or shared. Curiosity, not pitch.
Days 31–60 Build the path — front door, mid door, back door
  1. Rewrite your Instagram bio, your homepage headline, and your Work Together page around the A. Present state first, then the invitation. One who, everywhere. Replace "What would you choose?" with the sentence your person would recognize themselves in.
  2. Lower the Circle of Joy price and reframe it as the front door. Its job is the room, not the revenue. End every session with the same next step: book a call, or come to the monthly event.
  3. Run your first monthly live event. Online, $11 or $22, themed from the A. Promote it in stories every day for ten days before, in the weekly email, and in DMs. End with a clear invitation to a call about 1:1. Count who came and who booked.
  4. Do a day-45 content review. Pull your top five reels by shares and retention. Make the next thirty in their shape.
  5. Move the Inside-Out Circle to the after-step. Stop promoting it to new people. Offer it at the end of 1:1 as the place to stay connected and come back for top-ups.
Days 61–90 Momentum — repeat, refine, and hold the price
  1. Run the second monthly live event. Same format, better invitation, and compare attendance and calls booked against the first. That comparison is your first real data on the mid door.
  2. Batch-film weekly and let your VA schedule. Keep the daily reel, the daily stories, the daily DMs, and the Friday review. By day 90 you'll have made around ninety reels and you'll know exactly which kind works.
  3. Fill in the scorecard and compare. Followers, reel views, DM conversations, calls booked, clients enrolled: days 1 to 30 against days 61 to 90. Bring it to a call.
  4. Hold the 1:1 price on every call. No package variations. If someone can't say yes, the answer is the front door, not a discount.
  5. Decide what to double. With ninety days of numbers, we choose together: more of what's working on reels, a bigger monthly event, or, if the messaging is proven, the first small test of paid traffic. Not before.
Before you go

One last thing

This is a roadmap, not a rulebook. The sequence matters: the who, then the bridge, then the daily reel, then the path. How it feels in practice is yours to shape. If something isn't landing, that's information. Bring it to me.

The core of it one more time. You have a philosophy and a point of view on living a joy-filled life, and it's beautiful. What it needs is a bridge into a specific group of people with a real, urgent need that your work solves. Meet them where they are first. Then show them what's possible. Put that message in front of new people every single day, give them one simple path to you, and price the back door like the transformation it is. That's the business. It's not fundamentally about a better strategy. It's about the person on the other end of it.

Some of this will feel slow in the first few weeks. Daily reels to a small audience is a quiet way to start. Stay with it. The learning compounds before the numbers do.

I believe in you, Liz. You have something tremendous and valuable to offer. Your presence, your point of view, and your perspective on the world are what we need more of. With a bit more focus on bringing that perspective to meet people where they are, there's a lot of potential in this business, and I look forward to seeing it grow. When you hit something, a reel that flops, a price that catches in your throat, a week where the rhythm slips, that's exactly when to reach out. Not after you've figured it out alone.
