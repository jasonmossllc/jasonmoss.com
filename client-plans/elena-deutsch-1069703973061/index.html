Section 03

The biggest opportunities I see

Cut the fat and free up cash with a reactivation sprint. Then bring far more of the right people in the door, nurture them with live events, make it easier to talk to you, and simplify the offers.

1. Cut the fat first

Before we build anything, we eliminate. There's a lot of complexity and bloat in expenses, team and software right now that absolutely does not need to be there, and in marketing activities that aren't moving the needle. One of the first things we're going to do is cut the fat and get this business down to a lean, simple model, with a select set of tools and a lean team to execute. It starts with your P&L, line by line. That's how we get back to profitability.

2. Run a reactivation sprint for cash

You have a pipeline of over 500 people you've already had sales conversations with, and a core group of past clients who've "probably been the bulk of my income over these last years." You also told me why this fits you: "Woo is winning others over, and leaving people a voice message or reaching out and calling them is really powerful." You don't even call it follow-up. You call it "love them up." And you told me it takes a long time, because you feel you have to look up the last conversation and write something personal every time.

So here's the move. We use AI to do semi-automated outreach to those people, with a compelling offer that opens the door back up for a conversation. Let's focus one of our first calls on building a tangible, practical strategy to do this that's aligned with your strengths. This could be a very high-leverage way to generate short-term cash flow. It's easy, it's a quick win on the front end, and we run it in parallel with the longer-term build, with the cash redirected into top of funnel.

3. Build a real top of funnel

This is the biggest gap in the business and the biggest opportunity. Google, your ACC listing and referrals bring you leads, but that's inbound you can't turn up. The activities that have actually put you in front of new people on purpose are speaking, guest podcasting, and this summer's collaboration with Nina's group. Reach is the core problem to solve, and it's one of the first things we solve together. A simple stack: a paid-traffic lead magnet, regular speaking engagements, and guest podcasting. That's the trifecta.

4. Nurture with live events

Your nurture channels right now are fairly passive, and your live events are inconsistent. What I've found in my own business is that live events are the single best way to nurture and convert an existing audience. Once someone is on my list, my core strategy is to get them to a live experience. We'll shape what that looks like for you together.

5. Lower the barrier to a conversation

You wrote: "Maybe introduce something like you do, like a 20-minute fit call that's not a sales call, so they could have an experience of me before we go deeper." I agree. Right now your booking page is likely creating too much friction to just get someone on a call. We move to a short connection conversation, 15 to 20 minutes, with minimal qualification, framed around value rather than as a sales call. Something like: I'll help you get clarity on your next move in your career. If she's a fit, you invite her to a part-two conversation. Some women aren't quite ready to buy yet. But after spending 15 or 20 minutes with you, they'd be far more likely to take that next step.

6. Simplify to one core offer

Section 05

Positioning & messaging

Your niche is right. Your message is generally strong. The main thing I'd reflect on is where the conversation starts.

Keep the niche where it is

Your niche is incredibly clear, specific and well thought out. Women lawyers in a practice they no longer want to be in. Keep the focus there.

There's one slight shift I agree with: going upstream. The more established lawyer. The partner. The woman who's been practicing 15 or 20 years and knows her time with her kids is "precious and fleeting." That's a slight refinement, not a change of niche.

I think the women lawyers who've already left their practice, and the entrepreneur market, are distractions. You wrote about the Lawyer Coach Circle: "I enjoyed that, but I don't think I really want to specialize in that." About entrepreneurs: "I don't really want to be a full-on business coach." You can always serve the occasional client on the back end who walks in wanting something a little outside your lane. But I wouldn't focus or broaden your front-end marketing to speak to those groups. It would dilute the potency and impact of the message for the women you're actually here to serve.

Your origin story

I loved getting to hear the origin story of your business. The client in big law who got very clear that she wanted "the benefit of unlimited vacation days and to leave her firm and go to a startup." And you, on Cape Cod that summer, so fired up that you were waking at 5:30 in the morning to "write love letters to my future clients, telling them that they could do this." I'm curious how much of it is in your marketing. Parts of your story are on your About page and in a recent LinkedIn post. But the client breakthrough and the love letters aren't on your site anywhere. Are you sharing that part? It's powerful, and reading it connects me back to the why behind your work. I think it's an important piece of your message.

Meet her in the pain first

Here's one question I'd reflect on. Is your ideal client oriented around alleviating a pain or achieving a desire? When you describe her, she's tired. Tired of the billable hour. Tired of the dread. Tired of carrying everyone else's burdens. That's pain.

Your homepage already meets her there. Right under the headline she reads "You deserve to wake up excited about your day. Not filled with dread." But a lot of your other messaging leans toward desire. The Fall Sunday Sprint: "Joy Starts Here. Join the 6-week Sprint to begin your days, and end the year with momentum, focus and connection." The podcast tagline reads "For Women Lawyers Searching for Freedom, Flexibility and Fulfilment," and your LinkedIn bio leans the same way. There may be a bit of a mismatch between how you're framing your message and where your ideal client actually is when she first finds you.

The goal of marketing is to meet people where they are first, then build a bridge from that place to where they want to go. I'm not saying all of your marketing needs to be negative. I'm saying the first touchpoint should focus more on the pain, if that's where she is. Then the conversation can elevate as she goes deeper with you, into your live events and your calls. You said it yourself on our sales call: she needs you to be "the person who's holding up the door that's open." We likely need to build a better bridge, and it's something for us to talk through together.

You told me she "doesn't want to be told, oh, you should try and love the law, because they've tried." If that's where she is, that may be where your first touchpoint needs to start.

Section 06

Offers & pricing

Two lanes. One stable core offer that pays the bills, and a flexible lane of small live experiences where your creativity gets to run.

What's here now

WILL Transform at $12K with you, or $5,997 with one of your coaches. WILL Evolve at $297 a month. WILL Start at $1,997. The seasonal woo shops at $297 to $497. The Relief Now Toolkit at $49. The Sunday Sprint at $997. The Mystery Circle Mastermind at $12K. Retreats at $6K to $7K. The summer bootcamp with Law Mamas at $1,997.

Every one of these came from a real idea. But the more I read through them, the more I see way too much complexity here. There are too many offers for the business you're trying to run.

Lane one: the core offer

I want one core high-ticket offer that stays stable on the back end and provides your cash flow and profitability. Given your low volume of current clients, I'm going to suggest that offer be one-to-one with you, still leveraging your WILL Transform curriculum as the core of the delivery.

You already have VIP clients one-to-one, one of them at $24,000 for the year. And you wrote that you "have fun when I am coaching the right-fit clients one-on-one."

What I'm seeing across the industry is that group programs are becoming more difficult to sell. In a world where people can get information and answers so easily from AI, the real value is proximity and intimacy. Since I made the transition to one-to-one, my return on ad spend has gone up considerably, and I've found one-to-one offers much easier to sell. And as you wrote: "When only one or two people come on a community call, it's such a downer to me."

Community doesn't disappear. It may be that community becomes more of a nurture and conversion play: regular live events that give people the group feel. Then the one-to-one is the core way you actually monetize. We'll go deeper on this together before anything changes.

Lane two: the entry experiences

This is where your variety lives. Low-friction, low-risk, sub-$100 live experiences for people already in your audience: workshops, live events, three-day or five-day challenges. You run these more often, as bridges into the core offer. They can vary widely depending on your mood, what's exciting for you, what feels alive, what you want to share. You already work this way: "Even if I do the Sunday Sprint, every year is different." Your seasonal woo shops, at $297 to $497, function more like mini programs right now. You wrote that you've had "very little success enrolling new people into this. It's typically people who are already in my world."

It's close to what you asked for: "clarity of my core offer as the central thing, and clarity for these seasonal pillars that I can build marketing around, as well as room for rest and divine inspiration." I think that's how we accomplish both stability and spontaneity.

One downsell is fine

I like the idea of a single downsell for the women the core offer doesn't make sense for, around $2,000, like WILL Start. Core offer plus one downsell.

What we'll sort together

The place I see the most complexity is the back end, especially the coach-delivered tier and WILL Evolve. Then the masterminds, the retreats and the Sunday Sprint. When we simplify the offer suite, we'll go through each one together. Strategic is your number one strength. You'll see paths I've missed, which is why we do this together. You wrote that you want to "give myself full permission to want what I want and not want what I don't want, and let some things go if they don't feel aligned." That's exactly the spirit of this.

The back end, once

It's absolutely worth going through your back end once, making sure the materials are being delivered correctly in Funnel Gorgeous. Section 07

Marketing & content strategy

You're spending way too much time nurturing and not nearly enough time getting in front of new people.

How I see your funnel

Top of funnel is reach: a paid-traffic lead magnet, speaking, guest podcasting. Middle of funnel is nurture: your emails, your podcast, your live events. Bottom of funnel is conversations. Your September numbers show how low traffic and awareness are right now: 718 people visited the website. YouTube added six subscribers. And in the LinkedIn data your team pulled, 176 posts produced three link clicks.

What to stop

SEO and GEO (trying to rank in Google and show up in AI answers), including the content Mica is helping generate for it, isn't worth your focus right now. More broadly, you can cut 80 percent of your current marketing activities. They're basically just a waste of time, because you're having the same conversation again and again with the same thousand people who are engaged with you. Your list is about 940 people, and about 130 of them are alumni and your coaches.

Top of funnel: the trifecta

  • A paid-traffic front end. A simple lead magnet with a steady ad budget behind it. Very easy to set up. All your marketing to date has been organic, and you've never run ads "because I think it feels like my income hasn't been stable enough."
  • Speaking. You have a talk with the National Association of Women Lawyers coming up, and you've spoken for the New York City Bar, the DC Bar, the Beverly Hills Bar and more. You wrote that you don't think speaking is "a great way for me to get a lot of clients, but it does increase my visibility, and I have gotten some clients through it." Visibility is the job here. Along with guest podcasting and the Law Mamas collaboration, it's what has actually put you in front of new people.
  • Guest podcasting. There are a lot of legal podcasts now and you've been on a bunch. You stopped because there's no system and, in your words, "Follow-up and follow-through are one of my real weaknesses." Podcast discoverability is virtually zero unless you get on other people's shows, so this is where the podcast work turns into reach. Both need a solid pipeline and workflow.

This is why I really like a paid-traffic lead magnet for you. It runs in the background on autopilot for the most part, and it becomes the steady foundation that brings in new leads every single day. That makes your emails, calls and live events far more powerful. With that foundation underneath you, you don't have to be militantly consistent with your emails and live events. You're not using them for reach. You're using them to nurture the people already in the door. Consistency and Discipline sit at the bottom of your list. This is why that's fine. Your middle of funnel gets to be as creative as you want.

Speaking and guest podcasting both fit how you're built. Woo is in your top five. And as a 6/2 in Human Design, you do your best work when you're invited in. A stage and a guest seat are both invitations.

Middle of funnel: emails like love letters

You wrote: "I'm also really, really proud that I publish weekly and I have published consistently for almost 10 years." You should be. I just want to change what you're writing. Right now the newsletter "takes a good chunk of my time each week," and it isn't converting well into clicks or purchases.

One email a week is fairly low. I'd move to three to five. But not newsletters. Love letters to your ideal clients. It's how WILL started, with those love letters on Cape Cod. Quick thoughts, perspectives, stories, stream of consciousness, written in ten minutes.

My sense is you're seeing email a bit too much like a corporation would. Right now it's a template Christina sets up, with set sections (a personal message, news, the WILL Win of the Week, the podcast), drafted Monday or Tuesday for a Friday send. I'd treat it as frequent touchpoints that build connection, intimacy, desire and trust, and help her feel seen, heard and understood. Then give her a regular invitation to take a next step: book a call, or come to a live event. You wrote that you're "constantly finding mistakes that my team has not caught." I don't think you need your team for this. Go into your CRM, write, and send. Every email is just you writing to a friend.

Middle of funnel: live experiences

You wrote that building community and running group calls is "one of my favorite things to do," and "I have fun on the Sunday Sprint calls. I have fun when I'm leading workshops." Connectedness and Woo are two of your top five, so this part plays to what you already love. My guess is that ramping up live experiences, alongside solving top of funnel, is the best path to more sales opportunities.

Bottom of funnel: the conversation

  • Record your calls. Your sales call process looks fine. But one or two in ten is significantly lower than I'd like, and I need more information to diagnose it. It could be broader market shifts. It could be energetic or mindset, and you named that yourself: "my mindset and my energy are hugely important variables in this." It could be something missing in the process. I understand the sensitivity with clients. But recording with an AI note-taker is the single best way I've found to see what's going on. Give me transcripts from even a few calls, and we'll spend a session diving into them together.
  • Follow up inside the window. In my own business's data, about 90 percent or more of buyers close within 14 days of a sales call. When women leave a call needing "time to think about it," you wrote, they "rarely convert after that." There's still opportunity in long-term follow-up with past buyers, even low-ticket ones. But following up long-term with people who haven't bought after the first 30 days has generally been wasted time. That's not to say we abandon the reactivation sprint. We just right-size it to focus on quality opportunities.

Section 09

Your 90-day action plan

Elimination first. Then the front end. Then the offers. That order matters. The connection call, the emails and the live events can slide earlier or later, and we'll place them together, with room for the "periods of rest and creation" you told me you need. What you're not doing matters just as much: no broadening the niche, no SEO or GEO, no counting on LinkedIn, Facebook or YouTube for reach, no team-written newsletter, no back-end rabbit holes.

Days 1–30 Cut the fat and free up cash
  1. Bring the P&L to our first call on Tuesday. Last month's revenue and expenses, itemized line by line, software included. We go through every line together, along with every marketing activity that isn't working, and cut what doesn't need to be there. Tool consolidation can go to a one-off Upwork hire.
  2. Decide the team, together. With the numbers in front of us. I think we likely need to cut 80 percent, down to you plus an OBM or higher-level VA. I know there's resistance here, and you'll sleep on it before anything is locked in.
  3. Walk through WILL Transform once. A small side note, not a project. You wrote that you "really need to dedicate 3 hours and just do that so that I feel more confident in the delivery." That's the whole job.
  4. Run the reactivation sprint. We design it together on one of our first calls, then you run it: AI-assisted outreach to past clients and high-intent leads who didn't buy, with an offer that opens the door back up. The cash goes into top of funnel spend.
  5. Start recording your sales calls. Keep the AI note-taker on, save the full transcript, and send me the first few. Then we look at them together.
Days 31–60 Build the front end
  1. Launch the paid lead magnet. We choose the magnet together on a call, then turn on paid traffic behind it, funded by the reactivation cash.
  2. Build the speaking and guest podcasting pipeline. Regular speaking engagements and guest podcast appearances, with a solid pipeline and workflow behind them. We shape it together on a call.
  3. Open the door to a conversation. Replace the long questionnaire and the hour-long call with a 15-to-20-minute connection call. Value-focused framing, minimal qualification, and a clear path to the part-two conversation.
Days 61–90 Simplify the offers and ramp up live events
  1. Simplify the offer suite. My suggestion is one core one-to-one offer on the WILL Transform curriculum, one downsell around $2,000, and sub-$100 entry experiences. We explore it together and go through Evolve, the masterminds, the retreats and the Sunday Sprint. You sleep on it before we lock it in.
  2. Ramp up live experiences. Run them more often than you do now, sub-$100 and built around whatever feels alive for you. We shape the first one together on a call. Invite your list and your new leads.
  3. Write to her like a friend. Three to five short emails a week, written by you, regularly inviting her to book a call or come to a live event. Not a newsletter, and not a schedule to grind through. Love letters, the way WILL started on Cape Cod, each written in ten minutes.
