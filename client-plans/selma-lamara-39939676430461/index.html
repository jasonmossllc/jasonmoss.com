Section 03

The biggest opportunities I see

If you focus only on a few things over the next year, focus on these. In this order.

1. Bring you, visibly, into your business

This is the highest-leverage move in the entire plan. People do not feel safe with logos. They feel safe with humans. And your whole body of work is about helping a woman feel safe in her own body again — which means the way you show up has to model that safety before a single word about it lands.

Right now someone arrives at your site, reads beautiful language about presence and safety, and never sees whose hands are doing the work. They scroll Instagram and find a profile photo of a logo. They open a reel and watch text fly across the screen with no face attached to it. The most persuasive asset you have is missing from every front door.

This isn't just a visibility move. It's also where the inner work begins. The fact that you've been running this business without putting yourself in it is information. Something in you has been protecting you from being seen. That something is the same something that's worth getting curious about on our calls.

2. YouTube as your visibility engine

Instagram doesn't fit your nervous system. You said it plainly in your assessment — "I'm not a social media person. I prefer writing content and teaching like one-to-many." "High-energy marketing doesn't feel like me." Trust that. Your system is telling you the truth.

YouTube is the opposite of Instagram. It rewards depth, not snappiness. It rewards teachers. It rewards the kind of woman who can sit with a camera and explain — slowly, clearly, with weight — how chronic symptoms actually work and what's underneath them. That is who you are. The channel is already shaped for you. You just haven't planted yourself on it yet.

YouTube is also evergreen. A reel disappears in 48 hours. A YouTube video keeps pulling people in for years. That's the version of visibility that compounds without you needing to be on. It rewards the woman who can publish thoughtful, search-optimized teaching once a week — and then go live her life. Which is exactly the version of visibility your nervous system can hold.

This is the same engine I used to build my first six-figure coaching business online. YouTube long-form, lead magnet, email list, occasional live event. It works because it lets a teacher teach. You are, in your bones, a teacher.

3. Sharpen the A-to-B transformation

The niche is clear. The outcome isn't yet.

Your offer page tells someone where they're going — "steadier and safer in your body again" — but it doesn't yet show them what they'll see when they get there. That phrase is true and good, but it lands quietly. It doesn't pull someone forward the way the right picture would.

We talked about this on our call. Is the promise that she'll feel better than she has in years? That she'll stop crashing after small activities? That she'll start to trust her body again? That she'll be able to say no without her body needing to enforce the no through illness?

I'm not asking you to promise something you can't promise. I'm asking you to paint a clearer picture of what's possible. You've been doing this work long enough to know what the other side actually feels like. Trust that, and write it out. The clearer the B, the fewer leads you need.

4. The internal work is the real work

Everything I just named has a tactical layer and an internal layer. They are not separate.

The reason you're not in your own content has an internal answer. The reason you can't sustain a posting rhythm has an internal answer. The reason you keep crashing has an internal answer. The reason you wonder whether to keep posting on Instagram even though you don't want to has an internal answer.

The activities themselves are simple. One YouTube video a week. One email a week. A few stories. The activity isn't the hard part. Your relationship to the activity is what determines whether the activity gets to happen.

This is what we'll do on our group coaching calls. The rescuer. The achiever. What fires them. What being visible feels like in your body. What the cycle of overgive and crash is actually protecting. Until we work that layer, no strategy will hold. The minute we do, the strategy starts running almost on its own.

Section 04

What needs to shift first

Before any tactic in here works, a few things have to change at the foundation. They're not to-dos. They're orientations.

Reduce, don't add

You wrote it in your assessment: "When I get overwhelmed by too many strategies, platforms, or conflicting advice, I tend to freeze, overthink, my health flares up, and I lose alignment with myself."

I'm taking that seriously. This plan is built on subtraction. Almost every recommendation in here is permission to stop doing something — not pressure to add. You're going to come out of the next ninety days doing fewer things than you're doing now, not more. The few things that remain are the ones that compound.

Your relationship to visibility

Right now, being visible feels like a push. Like something you have to force yourself into. The Instagram posting feels heavy. The masterclass plan feels heavy. The WhatsApp content felt heavy by the end.

The activity itself isn't heavy. A YouTube video once a week is twenty minutes of speaking to a camera. The weight isn't in the doing. It's in your relationship to being seen.

This is what we work on in our calls. There is a version of visibility that doesn't drain you. It exists. I've watched a lot of people who started where you are find it. We're going for that one — not the version that has you forcing yourself onto a platform that isn't yours.

The rescuer, the achiever, and the cycle they make together

You named them both. The rescuer over-gives. The achiever takes over and pushes. Together they make output. Then your body says no. Then you crash. Then you come back and try again.

This pattern doesn't get solved by working harder. It gets solved by working differently. By designing the structure of the business so it matches the nervous system you're trying to build — not the one that's been driving the burnout.

That's why this plan looks the way it does. One channel. One simple rhythm. Built so a medium-health week can carry it. Built so the structure protects you, even on the weeks willpower can't.

The recorded masterclass — pause it

You mentioned in your assessment that you're focusing on building one, and you asked for my thoughts. Here they are: pause it.

Not forever. Just for now. Live trainings and masterclasses are powerful conversion tools — but they only work when you have an audience to put them in front of. Right now your audience is small. If you spend six weeks building a beautiful masterclass and the room is mostly empty when you finally run it, that crashes you in exactly the way we're trying to avoid. The disappointment alone could pull you back into the cycle for weeks.

We build the audience first. The masterclass comes later — in six to nine months — when there's someone there to receive it.

WhatsApp as a cold-audience funnel — let it go

WhatsApp is great for warm people. Paying clients. Paid challenge attendees. A small inner circle that's already bought in.

For cold followers, it asks too much. Switch platforms. Commit to a community. Expose your phone number to a stranger. People don't make that leap, and you said it yourself — the community never converted, even after a long time of sharing into it.

We start by meeting people where they actually are. On the platform → onto your email list → into a conversation. We don't ask them to leap into a chat group with a stranger before they've felt who you are.

This plan does not need more discipline from you. It needs less force. The version of this business that lasts is the version you can sustain on a day when your health is medium.

Section 06

Offers & pricing

The offer is good. Leave most of it alone. Make two clean cuts.

Inside Out — leave the spine alone

Twelve sessions over three months, £2500, a private 1:1 container. That's a well-built offer. The structure is right. The depth is right. The price is right for the work and for who you are. Don't dilute it. Don't second-guess it.

Spend your energy on getting more of the right women in front of it — not on rebuilding what's already working.

Drop the WhatsApp async support between sessions

You already decided this. I'm endorsing it.

You wrote in your assessment that the constant back-and-forth between sessions was burning you out, and you wondered whether it was a boundaries issue. It is — and it's also a design issue. You can have strong boundaries and still build an offer that pulls you out of them by default. The structure has to do some of the work, so your willpower doesn't have to do all of it.

The sessions are the work. The post-session summary and the agreed-on plans and resources — that's the bridge between sessions. That's enough. Cutting async support is a sustainability cut, not a quality cut. Your client will get the same depth of work. You'll arrive at each session with a fuller tank.

75-minute sessions → 60 minutes

75 is awkward. It doesn't fit cleanly into anyone's calendar — yours or theirs. Most people block their days in 30 or 60-minute increments. 75 sits halfway between, costs people more than they expect, and on your end it adds 15 minutes of energy expenditure across every session you run.

60 minutes is the standard for a reason. People can hold a 60-minute session. You can hold a 60-minute session. The depth doesn't suffer — it sharpens. You'll feel the difference within three or four weeks of session days. Less drained. Cleaner energy in and out.

If you ever feel a particular client needs longer, you can choose to extend a single session in the moment. Don't bake the extension into the structure.

The £2500 price point — hold it

You moved from £1500 to £2500 for the right reasons. The work is worth it. The credentials are worth it. The container is worth it.

The question isn't whether the price is right. The question is whether enough qualified women are seeing the offer at all. That's a top-of-funnel question. Not a pricing question. Don't drop the price as a response to slow sales — that's solving the wrong problem.

Post-container continuation

The one-off session for past clients is fine. A second 12-week container is fine. Keep both open. Don't over-build this yet — when more clients finish the full container, you'll see what they actually want. Build to demand, not to theory.

Section 07

Marketing & content strategy

One channel. One simple rhythm. Built for a woman who needs the business to hold her — not the other way around.

YouTube as the visibility engine

One to two long-form videos per week. Start with one. If one is sustainable for a full month, then move to two. If one starts to feel like a stretch, stay at one. The number is not the point. The rhythm is the point.

Each video is built around a specific search term your woman is actually typing into Google or YouTube. Things like:

  • Why am I crashing after small activities?
  • Chronic fatigue and the nervous system explained
  • What does it mean when all my tests come back normal?
  • Trauma stored in the body — what it actually is
  • The push-and-crash cycle in chronic illness
  • How a nervous system stuck in survival keeps you sick

These aren't viral hooks. They're problems your woman is searching for at 2am after another flare. When she searches one of those phrases and finds an eight-minute video of a Former NHS Doctor explaining the answer — calmly, clearly, with the depth you bring — she stops scrolling. She watches. She subscribes. She finds the link to your guide. She becomes a lead.

SEO-optimized titles. Thumbnails built to pull her in. You teaching to camera. Calm, grounded, not performative — the energy you already have. The camera just needs to see it.

Why this fits you specifically

You wrote, "I'm not a social media person. I prefer writing content and teaching like one-to-many."

YouTube is exactly that. It's teaching one-to-many in long-form. It rewards exactly the kind of work you do. The same instinct that makes Instagram feel wrong for your nervous system is the instinct that's going to make YouTube feel like an exhale once you find the rhythm.

Reels are short, fast, surface-level. They will not let people experience you. YouTube will. A woman who watches twelve minutes of you teaching feels she's met you. That's the difference between followers and clients.

The lead magnet — you already have it

"Your tests are normal. But your body clearly isn't." That guide is your one and only lead magnet. Don't make another one. Don't second-guess it. It's already a great hook for the right woman.

The job now is to pitch it in every single video. End every YouTube video the same way: "If this is landing for you, I have a free guide called 'Your Tests Are Normal' — link in the description." Same on Instagram and Facebook stories. Same on the website. Same in the YouTube channel banner. Same in the welcome sequence.

One lead magnet. Everywhere. Always. Until you have enough volume on the email list to need a second one — which won't be for a long time.

Email

Once someone is on the email list, they should be hearing from you regularly. Start at one email per week. If one is sustainable for a month, move to two.

The email is a reflection — the kind of writing you said feels most you. Not a polished marketing newsletter. Not a list of tips. A thoughtful piece of writing, a paragraph or two of teaching, a story from your work or your life, an idea you've been sitting with. The kind of email a thoughtful friend would receive in their inbox and read all the way through.

This is the same kind of writing you used to share into your WhatsApp community. Rerouted to a channel that actually grows and that meets a woman in her own quiet inbox, on her own terms.

Stories and posts (low effort, deliberate)

Light stories on Instagram and Facebook — a short reflection, a clip from a recent YouTube video, a photo from a walk, a passage from a book you're reading. Not produced. Not scripted. Brief moments that let the people who follow you between videos feel your presence.

For Reels — don't write new ones from scratch. Pull 60–90 second clips from your YouTube videos and repurpose. Two or three a week, max. Low effort, real consistency. If you ever stop wanting to do them, stop. Stories alone are enough for now.

Live trainings and masterclasses — later, not now

A live training or a monthly live event becomes a conversion mechanism once your audience is big enough to fill the room. Right now your audience is small, and a live event to a small room costs you more than it returns. You spend the energy, you brace for the disappointment, and the disappointment lands in your nervous system harder than the failure of any reel ever would.

So pause the masterclass plan. We come back to live conversion events at month six or nine — once the YouTube channel has built a base, the email list has grown, and a small live event has actual people in it.

This is the same strategy I used to build my first six-figure coaching business online. YouTube long-form, lead magnet, email list, occasional live event. It works because it lets a teacher teach — and you, in your bones, are a teacher.

Section 08

Systems & operations

The systems work here is mostly subtraction. Take things off the plate so the few things that matter can actually get done.

Pause everything you don't need

Three things come off the plate today.

  • The recorded masterclass project — close the tab, move the files into a "later" folder.
  • The public-facing WhatsApp community — stop directing strangers into it. Keep it only for paying clients if you want.
  • The pressure to be on Instagram in any way that resembles "Instagram marketing" — stories and YouTube repurpose are enough. The rest, off.

Each of these is a draw on your attention right now. When you take them off, the energy comes back for the few things that matter.

One weekly rhythm

The entire content engine fits in three lines:

  • One YouTube video
  • One email
  • A handful of light stories

That's it. Build it so a medium-health week can carry it. On a high-health week you can do more if you want. On a low-health week you can rest. The structure does not require maximum-you all the time. That's the whole design.

Email automation

The one piece of automation that matters: the lead magnet flow.

Someone signs up for "Your tests are normal" → they get the guide → they roll into a 5–7 email welcome sequence over the following two weeks → at the end of that, they roll into your weekly newsletter. The welcome sequence is your story, your work, who you help, the transformation painted out, and an invitation to apply to work with you. You write it once. It runs forever, in the background, while you're living your life.

Use whatever email platform you already have. Don't go shopping for a new one.

Boundaries as design, not willpower

Every system change in this plan is also a boundary change. The 60-minute session. Dropping async support. One channel of visibility instead of three. One lead magnet instead of a library. One simple weekly rhythm.

Each of those is a structural decision that protects you without requiring you to be disciplined in the moment. That's the difference between a business that depends on willpower and a business that holds you. Your nervous system has been doing a lot of work to compensate for design gaps. We're letting the design carry more of it from here.

Don't restructure your tools

You don't need new software. You don't need a fancier CRM. You don't need to migrate platforms. Whatever you have, use it.

Tools are not the bottleneck. Consistency is the bottleneck, and consistency comes from rhythm — not from software. A sharper tool pointed at the wrong rhythm just gets you to the wrong place faster.
